# Scenario 12 — Capstone: End-to-End Supply Chain Day

## Story

It's a normal Tuesday at your organization. A shipment from a supplier arrives in the morning. A clinic needs a resupply by the afternoon. A cycle count turns up a discrepancy. And right before closing, a quality alert comes in on a lot you shipped last week. This capstone runs the whole cast of roles through a single connected day, using the individual skills built in Scenarios 01–11.

Run this as a **group exercise** with one trainee per role, ideally people who will actually hold that role on the job. It works best after everyone has completed their individual scenario(s) at least once.

## Objectives

- Demonstrate that each role can perform their part correctly *and* hand off cleanly to the next role.
- Surface the coordination points where real warehouses actually break down (a shipment nobody picked up, a hold nobody documented, a count nobody actioned).
- Give trainers a single session to observe the whole team and identify who needs more one-on-one practice on which scenario.

## Roles needed

Assign one trainee to each: Buyer, Purchase Approver, Receiver, Stocker, Order Clerk/Requestor, Requisition Approver, Picker, Packer, Shipment Clerk, Manager. (Small teams can double up roles exactly as they would in real life — that's a valid and useful test too.)

## The day, in order

1. **08:00 — Inbound arrives.** The Buyer's PO from last week (created following Scenario 05) is delivered. The **Receiver** checks it in, deliberately under-receiving one line item to simulate a shortage, with a comment explaining why (Scenario 05 + 08 mechanics).
2. **09:00 — Putaway.** The **Stocker** puts the received stock away into the correct bins, including at least one item into the cold-chain zone set up in Scenario 02 (Scenario 06).
3. **10:00 — Quality alert.** A "supplier notice" arrives (the trainer announces a lot number to place on hold). The **Manager** and **Receiver** move that lot into the hold bin and document why (Scenario 11). The team should notice, and discuss, if that lot had already been picked for anything today — if so, what now?
4. **11:00 — Facility request.** The **Order Clerk** at the "clinic" creates a stock movement request against the warehouse for products including one *not* on hold and confirms it's within reasonable quantities. The **Requisition Approver** reviews and approves it (Scenario 07, Part A).
5. **13:00 — Pick, pack, ship.** The **Picker** works the picklist (noting that the held lot correctly does not appear as available), the **Packer** confirms contents, and the **Shipment Clerk** sends it (Scenario 07, Parts B–C).
6. **14:00 — Cycle count.** The **Manager** assigns a bin for a surprise cycle count. Whoever counts it enters the result — if the trainer has quietly altered the seeded quantity beforehand, this should surface a real discrepancy for the trainee to reconcile with a properly reasoned adjustment (Scenario 10).
7. **15:00 — Receiving at the clinic.** The shipment sent in step 5 now appears as inbound at the clinic. The clinic's **Receiver** processes it (Scenario 08 mechanics), including handling any transit issue the trainer wants to introduce (e.g. announce that one box arrived crushed).
8. **16:00 — Debrief.** Walk through the day as a group using the discussion prompts below.

## Group debrief prompts

- Where in the day did a role have to wait on another role? Was that hand-off clean, or did someone get stuck not knowing what to do next?
- Pick one document (the PO, the stock movement, the cycle count) and trace its full history from creation to its current status — can everyone in the room read that history and understand what happened, even the parts they weren't personally involved in?
- If the quality hold in step 3 had been discovered *after* the pick in step 5 instead of before, what would the team have needed to do differently?
- What's the single thing that would have gone wrong today if any one scenario (01–11) had been skipped during individual training?

## Success criteria

A cohort is ready to run this in production when the capstone completes with:

- No step requiring the trainer to explain what button to click (only *why*, when asked).
- Every discrepancy (the shortage, the cycle count difference, the crushed box) is documented with a real reason, not left blank or glossed over.
- The held lot never appears as pickable at any point in the exercise.
- Every trainee can explain, in one sentence, what the role *before* them and the role *after* them in the chain is responsible for.
